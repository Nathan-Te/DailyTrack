The French version is the reference (la version française fait foi): Mentions légales et confidentialité. This page is a faithful translation of it.

Du Jour (playdujour.com): DailyTrack and DailyJump. Last updated October 31, 2026.

  • No account, no email, no password: your profile is an identifier drawn at random by your browser, plus a nickname you choose.

What the server keeps

Your profile only exists on the server after your first ranked finish: before that, the server keeps nothing about you except the rate counters described below (hashed IP address, a few minutes).

  • Your secret identifier

    128 bits drawn at random by your browser, with no account or email. It is what your transfer code contains (your Du Jour profile): keep it like a password.

    Purpose: linking your times across your devices and across the games.

    Kept: until you ask for erasure.

  • Your nickname

    The one you choose; it is reserved for its first owner and shown in the leaderboards.

    Kept: until you ask for erasure (it is then released).

  • Your old, replaced codes

    When you change your code, the old one is refused from then on: the server only keeps a fingerprint of it (SHA-256), never the code itself.

    Kept: until you ask for erasure.

  • Your results and your replays

    For each day: your best time and its replay (the sequence of your inputs, which the server replays to check the time), in DailyTrack and in DailyJump, and your rank once the day is over. A fingerprint of these inputs is kept with it (a short code with nothing personal in it), used to refuse the same run being sent by two players, and, in DailyTrack, the car you made this time with (the model, its colour, its pattern and the pattern colour: a cosmetic choice, unrelated to the replay).

    Purpose: the daily and archive leaderboards, the leader's ghost (with the car of their run), your medals, your best rank, your streak and your perfect days (portal and shared profile).

    Kept: until you ask for erasure (past leaderboards remain viewable).

  • Your car in DailyTrack

    The car you pick in the Garage, with its colour, its pattern and the pattern colour, so that you find it again on all your devices (those that have your code). The server keeps only this last choice and the time you made it, to the minute.

    Kept: until you ask for erasure.

  • Your Room races

    Your best time of the session, its replay, your nickname and the car you made it with.

    Kept: forgotten 20 seconds after the end of the session (a session lasts 10 minutes).

  • Your deaths in DailyJump

    At most 30 per day: the replay of the life and the place where it ends.

    Purpose: the map of everyone's deaths and the “carnage”.

    Kept: forgotten at the end of the day (midnight, Paris time, plus 10 minutes). All that then remains is the map (the number of deaths per tile, with nobody attached) and a sample of about 150 lives (at most 3 per player), with no identifier or nickname, impossible to link to you.

  • Your IP address, hashed only

    To limit the number of submissions (against abuse), the server counts requests per IP address and per identifier: it only keeps a salted fingerprint (SHA-256 with a secret salt), never the address in clear. Some reads are only counted in memory, never written.

    Kept: about 40 minutes, then erased along the way; Room presence: 10 minutes; delay between two Room submissions: 1 hour.

  • The server's technical logs

    Start-up, errors, accepted time (day, time), refusals: never a nickname, an identifier, an IP address or a replay.

    Purpose: knowing whether the service works.

    Kept: 30 days (Google Cloud's default log retention).

  • Database backups

    Before each update of the site, a full copy of the database (everything described above) is made, in a private Google Cloud storage space (Belgium), readable only by the publisher and by the account that makes it.

    Purpose: being able to repair the database if an update damaged it.

    Kept: each copy is deleted after 30 days. After an erasure request, your data therefore also disappears from the copies within that time at the latest.

  • Site administration

    The administration panel (access reserved to the publisher, signing in with their Google account) keeps: their session (a random identifier and a fingerprint of their account, never their address), the log of their actions (time, action, nickname of the player concerned, what changed; for a profile deletion, only the nickname, never the erased content), server errors (a code, the route, the time: no player, no address, no request content) and the results they removed from a ranking (the time, the reason and the replay). Players have no access to this panel.

    Purpose: running the service (fixing a nickname, removing a result, following the state of the site) and being able to find what was done.

    Kept: the publisher's session lasts 1 hour; their log is kept 365 days; server errors 14 days; the replay of a removed result 30 days (the time and the reason then stay with the removed result, until the profile is erased).

  • And nothing else

    The rest of the database is not about players: the daily tracks and levels, the schedule, the database version, and the texts the publisher writes for everyone (the updates and the live messages: no player is named in them), and the state of each game (closed, preview or open) with the fingerprint of its preview key, never the key itself.

What the hosts see

As with any site, Google (which serves the pages and the server) receives your IP address with every request:

  • Cloud Run keeps a log of the requests received by the server (IP address, time, requested address — for some DailyTrack reads, it contains your identifier —, browser), kept 30 days; we only read it to diagnose an outage or an abuse;
  • Firebase Hosting uses IP addresses to detect abuse and keeps them “a few months” (according to Google: firebase.google.com/support/privacy).

The database is at Turso (a company established in the United States), in the region chosen when it was created.

The sign-in page of the administration panel, which players have no reason to open, loads Google's sign-in script (Google Identity Services): Google then sees the visit to that page. No other page of the site loads an external resource. That same panel reads GitHub's public API from the publisher's browser (the list of changes to the site, to write the updates): no player data goes there.

Links to other sites

The footer of the portal offers two links: the Du Jour Discord server and the publisher's Ko-fi page (support is always voluntary). They are plain links: the site loads no script, button or image from these services (the cup on the Ko-fi button and the pictogram on the Discord button are copies hosted on the site), and sends them nothing. You only leave Du Jour if you click; Discord and Ko-fi then apply their own rules (account, cookies, payment), which we have no hold over.

Music

The Du Jour games play music. The tracks are files hosted on the site (no outside music service, no outside script); they are downloaded only after your first gesture on the page (a tap, a click, a key), never before. The site neither keeps nor sends anything about what you listen to. Your music volume and the choice to mute it are kept in your browser with your settings (cdj:musique); the position of the track being played is kept for the length of your visit in the tab, so that the next page picks up in the same place (cdj:musique:reprise, tab storage, erased when you close it). What your browser keeps

The game keeps in your browser (local storage, never a cookie): your profile (identifier, nickname), your language (French or English, if you chose it, on the portal or in a game: shared by the portal and the games), your settings (sound, music, camera, controls), your DailyTrack car (the model, its colour, its pattern and the pattern colour, with the cars you have unlocked and the ones you have already seen: cdj:track:garage), the ghosts you ticked in the daily leaderboard (the public reference of their run, their nickname and their rank, forgotten at the next circuit) and the last ghost chosen with the button (cdj:track:fantomes), the graphics tier chosen for your device, with the date of the last smoothness measurement and its result (cdj:qualite), whether you dismissed the full-screen tip on iPhone, the fact that you skipped “Get my profile back” when opening the app added to your Home Screen (cdj:retrouver:vu; your transfer code is read from the clipboard only when you tap “Paste my code”), the last live message you dismissed (cdj:annonce:fermee) and the last update you read (cdj:nouveautes:lu), if you were sent a preview link to a game that isn't open yet, the preview key on this device (cdj:apercu:<game>), the number of the data generation your browser has seen (cdj:generation) and, once, the fact that a “Fresh start” message is to be shown (cdj:nouveau-depart) — they let the site start from zero when all its data is reset —, your records and your history, the memory of the day's game, pending submissions when you are offline, and for DailyTrack the archive track thumbnails. Keys cdj:… (DailyTrack and the shared profile) and jump:… (DailyJump).

This storage is strictly necessary for the game you ask for (without it: no profile, no settings, no records): it is therefore allowed without asking for your consent, and the site shows no banner (article 82 of the French Data Protection Act: trackers “strictly necessary for the provision of an online communication service at the express request of the user”; CNIL guidelines, deliberation no. 2020-091 of 17 September 2020, which also cover local storage). No tracker or third-party cookie, no ads, no audience measurement today. You can erase everything at any time by clearing the playdujour.com site data in your browser.

Legal basis

The publisher's legitimate interest (article 6.1.f of the GDPR): running the game and its leaderboards, which you choose to use by sending your times, and protecting it against abuse. No data is sold, none is used for advertising or profiling.

Your rights

  • Access: your nickname, your statistics and your medals are in your Du Jour profile (the portal's avatar, or Settings › “My Du Jour profile” in a game); for the rest, write to us.
  • Rectification: change your nickname in your Du Jour profile.
  • Erasure and objection: write to mzc.studios.gamedev@gmail.com with your transfer code (your Du Jour profile › “Copy my code”): it is the only proof that the profile is yours. We erase your profile, your times and replays, your deaths and your races, and your nickname is released; in past leaderboards your line disappears (the next ones move up one place). Answer within one month.

If you believe your rights are not respected, you can lodge a complaint with the CNIL (the French data protection authority): www.cnil.fr/fr/plaintes, or by post: CNIL, Service des plaintes, 3 place de Fontenoy, 75007 Paris, France.
